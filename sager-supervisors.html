Purpose and boundaries

Use ordinary supervisory conversations to establish whether candidates understand, own and can defend their research decisions when AI has assisted the work.

AI as a tool—and when it shapes research knowledge

AI can support research as a tool for bounded tasks, such as searching, organising information, formatting references or helping with code. It can also generate substantive content—analysis, explanations, interpretations or claims—that may shape the knowledge presented as the research contribution.

Your role is to establish that the candidate has produced and owns the research contribution, rather than simply carrying AI-generated ideas or interpretations into the thesis. They should be able to explain and justify their decisions, verify claims against original evidence, reproduce the analysis or trace the process where appropriate, and stand behind the conclusions.

SAGE-R provides a practical process for checking human ownership and verification. It is designed to fit within candidature milestones and supervisory checks already in place, strengthening the assurance you already provide without adding a separate stage of assessment.

How Defend works

Defend asks candidates to explain what AI contributed, justify important choices, verify results using evidence beyond AI, and stand behind the resulting scholarly claim. It can be a question in a meeting, a discussion of a draft, or a request to check a finding against its sources or data.

The assurance question: Can the candidate explain the work, justify the decisions, verify the evidence independently and take responsibility for the claim?

Tool identity alone does not establish research integrity. Supervisors may also ask which tools were used, how they were used and how outputs were checked, particularly where disclosure is required.

SAGE-R is designed to add no separate meeting or assessment: supervisors use these questions within ordinary interactions and existing candidature checks. Local implementation can align any notes with current recordkeeping practice.

Assurance across the pathway

These assurance points combine formal processes with ordinary supervisory activities. Their timing should follow the applicable candidature and ethics requirements; they are not eight mandatory sequential milestones.

Prospective / pre-enrolment

1. Research Proposal (Pre-Enrolment)

The applicant has not yet entered a formal supervisory relationship, and SAGE-R governance does not apply in the same way it governs enrolled candidates. The Defend function here is prospective. Publicly available SAGE-R guidance informs applicants that a research proposal must reflect their own research thinking — AI may assist with understanding how proposals are structured or what a research gap looks like, but the problem identified and the motivation expressed must be genuinely their own. A proposal substantially assembled by AI may result in a student enrolled into a project they do not intellectually own, which creates a capability formation problem from the first day of candidature.

Supervisor action

Use the first substantive meeting after enrolment as the Defend moment. Ask the student to state the research problem in their own words and explain why they chose it.

Supervisory activity

2. Foundation Work (Post-Enrolment)

The intellectual work of this stage — narrowing the problem, formulating research questions, selecting a methodological approach, and understanding ethical obligations — is foundational to the candidate's capability. The Defend function is embedded in supervisory meetings.

Supervisor action

Ask the candidate to articulate their research questions without AI-produced text, justify methodological choices using literature they have read directly, and explain the ethical considerations relevant to their study. Where appropriate, use existing supervisory notes to record the explanation; agree any additional recordkeeping, access or retention arrangements through institutional consultation.

Existing milestone preparation

3. Confirmation of Candidature Document

The COC carries two distinct dimensions under SAGE-R. Part A — research questions, methodology, conceptual framework and expected outcomes — is where the student’s ownership of the research must be clearest, and every substantive claim must be defensible by the student without AI mediation. Part B — budget, Gantt chart, risk register, occupational safety and procedural ethics elements — is more administrative. AI assistance with Part B may be appropriate where permitted, provided the student understands and approves every element and uses approved tools and data-handling arrangements for sensitive content.

Supervisor action

Before submission, hold a targeted Defend conversation: why these research questions, why this methodology, what the conceptual framework means, and what the expected original contribution is. This accompanies supervisory feedback and editorial review; it does not replace them.

Existing examination process

4. COC Examination

CQU sends the COC to two independent examiners. This is already a formal external Defend moment. If AI-assisted drafting has introduced claims or positions the student cannot explain, examiner scrutiny may identify this.

Supervisor action

Before the COC is submitted, check that the student can answer examiner-level questions about every substantive element of the document, including claims or positions shaped with AI.

Where ethics approval is required

5. Ethics Application

Ethics applications are procedurally dense and often poorly understood by students at the outset. AI assistance in navigating what a section is asking, organising a response, or converting the student's reasoning into institutional form may be appropriate where permitted. Do not upload ethics documentation, participant information or confidential project details unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved. The ethical reasoning itself — what risks exist, how they are mitigated, what participants will experience, and why the study design is ethically defensible — must be the student's own.

Supervisor action

Before submission, ask the student to justify each substantive ethical position in their own words, including the risks identified, how they will be mitigated, and what participants will experience.

Existing progress review

6. Progress reporting and review

Progress reports provide a longitudinal opportunity to see how the candidate's scholarly understanding develops across candidature.

Supervisor action

Within the existing report review, ask a few targeted questions about recent work: why a particular analytical decision was made, what a finding means, and how an AI-assisted section was verified. Any notes can follow current recordkeeping practice.

Supervisory assurance activity

7. Pre-submission readiness

A final readiness check helps establish that the thesis is understood and owned by the student before external examination, where supervisors cannot contextualise the candidate's capability for examiners.

Supervisor action

Choose a small sample of references, claims and analytical interpretations. Ask the student to verify and justify them against the original source material or dataset.

Conversation prompts

Choose questions that fit the candidate’s discipline and current work. Ask them to connect the explanation to an actual source, dataset or design decision.

Establish ownership

  • Why did you choose this research question?
  • What alternatives did you consider?
  • Which decisions were yours, and why?

Make verification visible

  • Show the evidence behind this claim.
  • What did the AI get wrong?
  • What changes if this assumption fails?
Example: discussing an AI-assisted analysis

“Walk me through why you selected this method. Show how you checked the output against your data. Which alternative explanation did you rule out, and what remains uncertain?”

Use the answer to identify understanding and any need for further verification or support. A polished answer alone is not a substitute for checking the evidence.

Proposed oral Defend

Proposed use within an existing review

8. Oral Defend conversation with the Supervisory Panel

A structured oral Defend conversation can be included in an existing supervisory-panel meeting or pre-submission review; SAGE-R does not require a separate meeting or additional checkpoint. Any notes should follow agreed institutional recordkeeping, access and retention arrangements. SAGE-R does not independently impose a new provenance-record obligation.

Supervisor action

If this conversation is adopted locally, use the existing panel review to ask how AI contributed and what was verified, which intellectual decisions were the student's own, and how the candidate would answer an examiner's challenge about a claim or methodological choice. Its use remains subject to institutional consultation and pilot evaluation.

Records and disclosure

SAGE-R does not itself require comprehensive prompt logs or additional declaration forms. Existing institutional, ethics, contractual, publisher or project-specific requirements may nevertheless require disclosure or retention of appropriate records.

Where appropriate, existing supervisory notes may record the candidate’s explanation and verification decisions. Any additional recordkeeping, access and retention arrangements should be agreed through institutional consultation.

Quick-reference checklist

Stage The Defend question What it looks like in practice
1. Research Proposal
Educational		 Did the student arrive with a research problem they genuinely own? First supervisory meeting: student explains the research problem in their own words and why they proposed it.
2. Foundation Work
Research Ownership		 Can the student articulate research questions, methodology, and ethical considerations without AI mediation? Supervisory conversation: student explains each element without reference to AI-produced prose.
3. COC Development
Substantive Assurance		 Does the student own every substantive claim in Part A? Pre-submission conversation: why these questions, why this methodology, what the framework means, what the contribution is.
4. COC Examination
Independent External		 Can the student explain and defend their research decisions to examiners who have not supervised the work? Supervisor confirms readiness before submission by verifying the student can address substantive examiner questions.
5. Ethics Application
Integrity Assurance		 Can the student justify every substantive ethical position in the application? Pre-submission check: student explains risk identification, mitigation, and consent design in their own words.
6. Progress reporting and review
Longitudinal Capability		 Has scholarly understanding grown across the reporting period? Has AI-assisted work been verified? Targeted questions during the report review meeting: analytical decisions, specific findings, verification of AI-assisted sections.
7. Pre-submission readiness
Verification Assurance		 Can the thesis survive random spot-checking of references, claims, and analysis? Supervisor randomly selects references and claims and asks the student to verify against source material or dataset.
8. Oral Defend in an existing panel review
Final Assurance		 Can the student account for the entire research process — AI contributions, verification decisions, and intellectual ownership? Include the structured conversation in an existing panel meeting or pre-submission review, subject to consultation and pilot evaluation: AI use, verification, intellectual ownership, and response to examiner-style challenge.

Before formal adoption, seek institutional consultation on policy fit, supervisory workload, recordkeeping and how the proposed Defend conversation fits within existing review processes.
