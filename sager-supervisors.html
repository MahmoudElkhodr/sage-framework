Purpose and boundaries

Use ordinary supervisory conversations to establish whether candidates understand, own and can defend their research decisions when AI has assisted the work.

SAGE-R is an independent scholarly framework developed by CQUniversity researchers. This working draft is for consultation and is not currently an official CQUniversity policy or procedure.

Policy and permission boundary

SAGE-R does not independently authorise the use of generative AI. Any use must comply with applicable University policies, research ethics approvals, data-management and privacy requirements, intellectual-property arrangements, funder and publisher conditions, disciplinary standards, and supervisory guidance. Only institutionally approved tools and data-handling arrangements should be used where required.

Protect research information

Protect research data and intellectual property

Do not upload identifiable participant information, confidential datasets, culturally restricted information, commercially sensitive material, unpublished intellectual property, ethics documentation or third-party content to an AI service unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved. De-identification alone may not be sufficient where data-sharing agreements, Indigenous data sovereignty, contractual restrictions or re-identification risks apply.

How Defend works

Defend asks candidates to explain, justify and stand behind their work. It can be a question in a meeting, a discussion of a draft, or a request to verify a finding against the underlying evidence.

The assurance question: Does the candidate understand this work well enough to explain it, justify it and stand behind it?

Tool identity alone does not establish research integrity. Supervisors may also ask which tools were used, how they were used and how outputs were checked, particularly where disclosure is required.

The framework is designed to minimise additional workload by using existing supervisory interactions. A pilot should evaluate workload and recordkeeping implications.

Assurance across the pathway

These assurance points combine formal processes with ordinary supervisory activities. Their timing should follow the applicable candidature and ethics requirements; they are not eight mandatory sequential milestones.

Prospective / pre-enrolment

1. Research Proposal (Pre-Enrolment)

The applicant has not yet entered a formal supervisory relationship, and SAGE-R governance does not apply in the same way it governs enrolled candidates. The Defend function here is prospective. Publicly available SAGE-R guidance informs applicants that a research proposal must reflect their own research thinking — AI may assist with understanding how proposals are structured or what a research gap looks like, but the problem identified and the motivation expressed must be genuinely their own. A proposal substantially assembled by AI may result in a student enrolled into a project they do not intellectually own, which creates a capability formation problem from the first day of candidature.

The Defend moment is the supervisor's first substantive meeting post-enrolment: the student should be able to articulate the research problem in their own words and explain why they chose it.

Supervisory activity

2. Foundation Work (Post-Enrolment)

The intellectual work of this stage — narrowing the problem, formulating research questions, selecting a methodological approach, and understanding ethical obligations — is the most foundational capability formation of the entire candidature. The Defend function is embedded in supervisory meetings. The student should articulate research questions without reading from AI-produced text, justify methodological choices by reference to literature they have read directly, and explain the ethical considerations relevant to their specific study design. Where appropriate, existing supervisory notes may document the candidate's explanation. Any additional recordkeeping, access and retention arrangements would need to be agreed through institutional consultation.

Existing milestone preparation

3. Confirmation of Candidature Document

The COC carries two distinct dimensions under SAGE-R. Part A — research questions, methodology, conceptual framework, and expected outcomes — is where epistemic ownership must be most clearly demonstrated, and every substantive claim must be defensible by the student without AI mediation. Part B — budget, Gantt chart, risk register, occupational safety, and procedural ethics elements — is more administrative. AI assistance with Part B may be appropriate where permitted, provided the student understands and approves every element and uses approved tools and data-handling arrangements for sensitive content.

Before submission, the supervisor conducts a targeted Defend conversation covering at minimum: why these research questions, why this methodology, what the conceptual framework means, and what the expected original contribution is. This replaces neither supervisory feedback nor editorial review — it accompanies them.

Existing examination process

4. COC Examination

CQU sends the COC to two independent examiners. This is already a formal external Defend moment. The supervisor's role before submission is to confirm that the student can respond to examiner-level questions about every substantive element of the document. If AI-assisted drafting has introduced claims or positions the student cannot explain, examiner scrutiny may identify this.

Where ethics approval is required

5. Ethics Application

Ethics applications are procedurally dense and often poorly understood by students at the outset. AI assistance in navigating what a section is asking, organising a response, or converting the student's reasoning into institutional form may be appropriate where permitted. Do not upload ethics documentation, participant information or confidential project details unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved. The ethical reasoning itself — what risks exist, how they are mitigated, what participants will experience, and why the study design is ethically defensible — must be the student's own. The Defend check before submission is a brief targeted conversation: the student justifies every substantive ethical position in the application in their own words.

Existing progress review

6. Progress reporting and review

Progress reports are the primary longitudinal Defend mechanism. The supervisor's oral review accompanying the written report should include a small number of targeted questions about the work produced during the reporting period: why a particular analytical decision was made, what a specific finding actually means, how an AI-assisted section was verified. These questions are intended to fit within existing supervisory conversations. A pilot should evaluate any additional meeting time and recordkeeping needs.

Supervisory assurance activity

7. Pre-submission readiness

Before submission, the supervisor conducts a structured spot-check: randomly selecting references, claims, and analytical interpretations from the thesis and asking the student to verify and justify them against the source material or dataset. The purpose is to confirm that the thesis is genuinely owned and understood by the student before it enters external examination, where no supervisory knowledge of the student's capabilities is available to contextualise the work.

Conversation prompts

Choose questions that fit the candidate’s discipline and current work. Ask them to connect the explanation to an actual source, dataset or design decision.

Establish ownership

  • Why did you choose this research question?
  • What alternatives did you consider?
  • Which decisions were yours, and why?

Make verification visible

  • Show the evidence behind this claim.
  • What did the AI get wrong?
  • What changes if this assumption fails?
Example: discussing an AI-assisted analysis

“Walk me through why you selected this method. Show how you checked the output against your data. Which alternative explanation did you rule out, and what remains uncertain?”

Use the answer to identify understanding and any need for further verification or support. A polished answer alone is not a substitute for checking the evidence.

Proposed oral Defend

Proposed additional checkpoint

8. Proposed informal oral Defend with Supervisory Panel

SAGE-R proposes an informal structured conversation with the supervisory panel before submission as an additional assurance checkpoint. This proposal would require institutional consultation and pilot evaluation before formal adoption and would complement the applicable examination process. Three questions anchor it: how AI contributed to the candidature and what was verified; which intellectual decisions were entirely the student's own; and how the student would respond to a specific examiner challenge on a claim or methodological choice. Any record of this proposed session should follow agreed institutional recordkeeping, access and retention arrangements; SAGE-R does not independently impose a new provenance-record obligation.

Records and disclosure

SAGE-R does not itself require comprehensive prompt logs or additional declaration forms. Existing institutional, ethics, contractual, publisher or project-specific requirements may nevertheless require disclosure or retention of appropriate records.

Where appropriate, existing supervisory notes may record the candidate’s explanation and verification decisions. Any additional recordkeeping, access and retention arrangements should be agreed through institutional consultation.

Quick-reference checklist

Stage The Defend question What it looks like in practice
1. Research Proposal
Educational		 Did the student arrive with a research problem they genuinely own? First supervisory meeting: student explains the research problem in their own words and why they proposed it.
2. Foundation Work
Epistemic Ownership		 Can the student articulate research questions, methodology, and ethical considerations without AI mediation? Supervisory conversation: student explains each element without reference to AI-produced prose.
3. COC Development
Substantive Assurance		 Does the student own every substantive claim in Part A? Pre-submission conversation: why these questions, why this methodology, what the framework means, what the contribution is.
4. COC Examination
Independent External		 Can epistemic ownership withstand scrutiny from examiners with no supervisory knowledge of the student? Supervisor confirms readiness before submission by verifying the student can address substantive examiner questions.
5. Ethics Application
Integrity Assurance		 Can the student justify every substantive ethical position in the application? Pre-submission check: student explains risk identification, mitigation, and consent design in their own words.
6. Progress reporting and review
Longitudinal Capability		 Has scholarly understanding grown across the reporting period? Has AI-assisted work been verified? Targeted questions during the report review meeting: analytical decisions, specific findings, verification of AI-assisted sections.
7. Pre-submission readiness
Verification Assurance		 Can the thesis survive random spot-checking of references, claims, and analysis? Supervisor randomly selects references and claims and asks the student to verify against source material or dataset.
8. Proposed informal oral Defend
Final Assurance		 Can the student account for the entire research process — AI contributions, verification decisions, and intellectual ownership? Proposed panel conversation, subject to consultation and pilot evaluation: AI use across candidature, decisions that were entirely the student's own, response to examiner-style challenge.

Before formal adoption, seek institutional consultation on policy fit, supervision workload, recordkeeping and evaluation of the proposed checkpoint.
