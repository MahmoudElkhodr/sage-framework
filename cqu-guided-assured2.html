Implementing CQUniversity’s Guided–Assured Assessment Model with SAGE

A practical page for Unit Coordinators preparing Unit Profiles, Moodle assessment instructions, tutorial activities, and assurance checkpoints before the start of term.

The institutional model classifies the assessment; SAGE operationalises the pedagogy.

Start here before term

Use the official CQUniversity Educator Guide to select the tier. Then use this page to turn that classification into student instructions and teaching activities.

  1. Classify each assessment as Tier 1, 2, or 3.
  2. Record the tier and permitted GenAI use in the Unit Profile.
  3. Build the SAGE process into Moodle and tutorials.
  4. For Tier 2, timetable the mandatory Defend component for every student.
Before selecting a tier

Start with the capability

The Guided–Assured Assessment Model begins with the learning outcomes. Define what students must be able to do, decide whether that capability should be demonstrated with or without GenAI, and select evidence that would validly demonstrate the learning.

1
What must students demonstrate independently?

Identify foundational, threshold, safety-critical, accreditation or progression capabilities that must remain personally internalised.

2
Where is GenAI authentic professional practice?

Identify capabilities where responsible GenAI use, critical evaluation, testing, adaptation and human accountability are themselves part of the intended learning.

3
What evidence would give confidence that learning occurred?

Choose evidence matched to the capability, such as independent performance, an unseen application, a live explanation, a working artefact, a practical demonstration or documented professional judgement.

A capability-first assessment-design sequence
Learning outcome → Required capability → Authentic role of GenAI → Evidence of learning → Guided–Assured tier → Conditions and controls
Risk is not ignored. It is considered after the capability, authentic role of GenAI and required evidence have been established. Unit Coordinators should then record the selected tier, permitted GenAI use and assessment conditions consistently in the Unit Profile and Moodle.

Step 1 — classify

The three Guided–Assured tiers

Choose the tier from the learning outcome and the level of assurance required. Do not begin by deciding whether AI is “allowed” or “banned”.

Tier 1

High-security (assured)

Use when students must demonstrate an essential capability independently, without GenAI assistance.

  • Single secured assessment component.
  • GenAI prohibited, restricted, or used only under direct observation.
  • State supervision, time, format, and permitted materials clearly.
SAGE role: formative learning before the assessment. The secured assessment itself verifies independent attainment.
Tier 2

Medium-security (guided assured)

Use when GenAI can support authentic preparation, but every student must still verify personal understanding in real time.

  • Lower-weighted guided component.
  • Higher-weighted mandatory interactive oral, viva, or equivalent live defence.
  • Student responses are unscripted; examiner challenge should be structured.
SAGE role: Steps 1–5 guide the open component. Step 6, Defend, provides the assurance checkpoint.
Tier 3

Low-security (guided)

Use for authentic professional tasks where GenAI is deliberately integrated and the learning focus is critical judgement and responsible use.

  • Select the applicable GenAI Guidance Level.
  • Require transparent process evidence and declaration.
  • Assess judgement, contextual adaptation, and disciplinary reasoning.
SAGE role: Steps 1–5 structure the assessed human–AI collaboration. Tier 3 is primarily a guided learning and authentic professional-practice design. It provides process transparency and evidence of judgement, but it does not by itself provide strong independent assurance of attainment. Where an essential capability requires independent assurance, verify it through a Tier 1 or Tier 2 checkpoint elsewhere in the unit or course.
Online delivery caution

An assessment does not become high-security merely because students complete it online during a scheduled class. Students may still have access to another device, off-camera assistance or a separate GenAI session. Unit Coordinators should consider whether the actual delivery conditions provide credible evidence of independent attainment. Where equivalent online assurance cannot be achieved, use layered controls, add an individual oral or performance component, redesign the task, or locate the assured checkpoint elsewhere in the course.

Course-level assurance check

Not every unit needs a Tier 1 or Tier 2 assessment. Equally, a course should not rely only on Tier 3 evidence where essential capabilities require independent assurance. Before adding or removing a secured checkpoint, confirm:

  • which essential learning outcomes require independent verification;
  • where each is assured across the course;
  • whether the checkpoint is positioned at an appropriate foundation, progression, accreditation or culminating stage; and
  • whether an existing checkpoint already provides sufficient assurance without unnecessary duplication.
Step 2 — set the capability target

SAGE student orchestration levels

The guidance level states what GenAI use is permitted. The orchestration level states the capability the assessment is designed to develop and the rubric may assess.

CQUniversity Guidance Level Wording in the Educator Guide Intended SAGE capability target Assessment emphasis
Guidance Level 1 — AI-Assisted Passive Acceptor → Selective Adapter Selective Adapter Students use GenAI for limited support, evaluate output against an authoritative anchor, and document meaningful accept, modify and reject decisions.
Guidance Level 2 — AI Integration Balanced Integrator Balanced Integrator Students combine human and AI contributions systematically, document refinement, and explain important trade-offs and contextual decisions.
Guidance Level 3 — Custom or Task-Specific AI Use Critical Synthesiser Critical Synthesiser Students customise AI-supported workflows, identify gaps, assumptions and bias, and develop a traceable synthesis or contextual adaptation beyond both starting inputs.
How this mapping interprets Guidance Level 1

The CQUniversity Educator Guide presents Guidance Level 1 as movement from Passive Acceptor to Selective Adapter. SAGE treats Passive Acceptor as the starting condition to be remediated, rather than a target or permitted endpoint, because the Evaluate and Refine steps require students to question AI output against an authoritative anchor and exercise visible human judgement. Accordingly, this implementation mapping identifies Selective Adapter as the intended capability target for Guidance Level 1.

Step 3 — operationalise

How to implement SAGE in your unit

A Unit Coordinator normally needs to make decisions in three places: the Unit Profile, Moodle, and the teaching schedule.

1
Before the Unit Profile closes

Confirm the tier, weighting, task components, permitted GenAI use, and what evidence students must provide.

2
Build the Moodle task

Add the classification, student instructions, SAGE process, declaration, marking criteria, and submission evidence.

3
Teach the process

Use tutorials to model a human starting point, evaluation anchors, accept/modify/reject decisions, AI Critic, and reflection.

4
Verify or assess

Tier 1 verifies independent capability. Tier 2 runs Defend for every student. Tier 3 assesses responsible GenAI orchestration.

📘 Put this in the Unit Profile

  • The Guided–Assured tier.
  • The GenAI Guidance Level where applicable.
  • Whether GenAI is prohibited, guided, or deliberately integrated.
  • The components and weighting, especially for Tier 2.
  • What students must submit and how learning will be verified.

🧭 Put this in Moodle

  • A short explanation of why the assessment uses this tier.
  • The relevant SAGE steps and discipline-specific anchors.
  • The process evidence, declaration, and reflection required.
  • The marking criteria for human judgement and contextualisation.
  • For Tier 2, the date, format, and expectations for Defend.

🧪 Build this into tutorials

  • Show one imperfect AI output and evaluate it together.
  • Provide an anchor: rubric, standard, reading, policy, or professional framework.
  • Practise accept, modify, and reject decisions with reasons.
  • Run a short AI Critic activity and audit its feedback.
  • For Tier 2, rehearse the type of reasoning students will defend.

✅ Check before release

  • The Unit Profile and Moodle use the same tier and weighting.
  • Students can see exactly what GenAI use is permitted.
  • The rubric rewards judgement, not prompt volume.
  • Tier 2 applies real-time verification to every student.
  • Online and accessibility arrangements are stated in advance.
Copy and adapt

Unit Profile and Moodle wording

Replace the bracketed text with the unit-specific task, evidence, weighting, standards, and dates.

Tier 1 — high-security (assured)
This is a Tier 1 high-security (assured) assessment. It verifies that each student can independently demonstrate [essential learning outcome/capability]. Generative AI is not permitted during this assessment. The task will be completed [in person/online under approved supervised conditions] during [date/time window]. Permitted materials are [list]. Students must submit the required work before the end of the assessment session and confirm that the work is entirely their own.
Tier 2 — medium-security (guided assured)
This is a Tier 2 medium-security (guided assured) assessment with two linked components. Part A [weighting] is the lower-weighted guided component. Generative AI may be used in accordance with the stated SAGE process, and students must submit [artefact/process evidence/declaration]. Part B [higher weighting] is a mandatory individual [interactive oral/viva/live technical demonstration with structured questioning]. Every student must explain, justify, critique, and extend the reasoning represented in the submitted work in real time. Generative AI is not permitted during Part B.
Tier 3 — Guidance Level 1 / Selective Adapter
This is a Tier 3 low-security (guided) assessment using GenAI Guidance Level 1. Generative AI may be used for limited support such as brainstorming, initial drafting, or basic research assistance. Students must evaluate the output against [rubric/readings/standards], record significant accept, modify, and reject decisions, correct errors or gaps, and include a short declaration and reflection. The assessment is designed to develop Selective Adapter capability rather than passive acceptance of AI output.
Tier 3 — Guidance Level 2 / Balanced Integrator
This is a Tier 3 low-security (guided) assessment using GenAI Guidance Level 2. Generative AI may be integrated as a collaborative professional tool for [planning/drafting/analysis/coding/workflow support]. Students must document how AI output was evaluated and refined, identify key trade-offs, show where personal and disciplinary judgement changed the work, and submit the required SAGE evidence and declaration. The assessment is designed to develop Balanced Integrator capability.
Tier 3 — Guidance Level 3 / Critical Synthesiser
This is a Tier 3 low-security (guided) assessment using GenAI Guidance Level 3. Students may design or customise task-specific prompts, tools, or multi-tool workflows for [assessment purpose]. They must proactively identify gaps, bias, and limitations; produce a traceable synthesis or solution extending beyond both the human starting point and the AI output; and reflect on the ethical, creative, and disciplinary implications of their decisions. The assessment is designed to develop Critical Synthesiser capability.
CQUniversity Tier 2

Using the SAGE Defend Tool correctly

The Defend Tool is sector-facing and intentionally broader than the CQUniversity Tier 2 definition. Use the following compatibility rules when implementing Tier 2 at CQUniversity.

Check whether Tier 2 is appropriate

Tier 2 should be selected because both components serve a clear pedagogical purpose, not merely as a compromise between allowing and prohibiting GenAI.

1
Is real-time verification feasible for every student?

Confirm staffing, timetable, delivery mode, accessibility, reasonable adjustments, rescheduling and moderation arrangements before committing to Tier 2.

2
Does the learning outcome lend itself to structured live verification?

The student should be able to explain, justify, apply, modify, demonstrate or extend the capability in real time.

3
Is there a pedagogical rationale for both components?

The GenAI-supported component should develop authentic professional capability, while the interactive component should verify individual ownership, understanding and disciplinary reasoning.

If the answer to one or more questions is no, consider Tier 1, Tier 3, redesign the task, or place the assured checkpoint elsewhere in the course.

Meets Tier 2 when applied to every student

  • Interactive oral assessment or viva voce.
  • Oral presentation followed by structured individual challenge.
  • Live code walkthrough or technical demonstration with questioning.
  • Design, scenario, or professional defence requiring real-time justification.
  • Synchronous online oral with appropriate identity and delivery controls.

Useful, but not sufficient by itself

  • Open presentation Q&A without deliberate individual challenge.
  • Sampled oral verification that does not include every student.
  • Process logs, declarations, or AI interaction records.
  • Invigilated exams, lab tasks, locked browsers, or remote proctoring without interactive verification.
  • Authorship quizzes or behavioural analytics used as stand-alone assurance.
Weighting requirement: the official model defines Tier 2 as a lower-weighted guided component followed by a higher-weighted mandatory interactive component for every student. Finalise the split before the Unit Profile is approved and use the same weighting in the Unit Profile, Moodle and marking documentation.

Illustrative arrangements include:

  • A 30% assessment: guided submission 10% and individual interactive component 20%.
  • A 40% assessment: guided submission 15% and individual interactive component 25%.
  • A 50% assessment: guided submission 20% and individual interactive component 30%.

These examples are illustrative rather than prescribed. The mandatory individual interactive component must carry more weight than the GenAI-supported preparatory component.

Plan the workload before finalising the Unit Profile

The following SAGE Defend Tool figures are indicative planning estimates, not institutional workload allocations. They help Unit Coordinators test whether the proposed format can be delivered to every student.

Format Indicative time per student Approximate throughput per examiner day Indicative design preparation
Oral presentation with targeted examiner questioning 8–12 minutes 20–30 students 1–2 hours to design outcome-mapped questions and a question-allocation matrix
Viva voce 10–15 minutes 15–20 students 2–3 hours for question design plus approximately 1 hour for examiner calibration
Interactive oral assessment 15–20 minutes 12–15 students 2–3 hours to prepare the question bank and calibrate assessors
Live practical demonstration 15–25 minutes 10–15 students 3–4 hours to prepare and verify unseen tasks, changed requirements or seeded errors

These estimates exclude administration, student transitions, breaks, reasonable adjustments, moderation, technical failures, rescheduling and marking reconciliation. Online delivery should also include a scheduling buffer. Where multiple examiners are used, calibration and comparable challenge must be planned explicitly.

Structured does not mean scripted: examiner questions should be prepared, mapped to learning outcomes, and applied consistently. Student explanations and responses must remain unscripted.
Why logs do not replace Defend: in a structured audit of 25 SAGE submissions, only 3 produced substantially auditable evidence chains. Process records support transparency and learning, but Tier 2 adds real-time verification because documentation alone is not conclusive evidence of individual attainment.
