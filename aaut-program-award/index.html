Overview

The educational problem and the SAGE response

Students were often told whether generative AI was permitted, but not how to use it responsibly. Policy and detection approaches alone do not teach students to verify AI output or retain ownership of their reasoning.

The gap

Permission settings answer whether AI may be used. They do not show students how to test claims against disciplinary evidence, revise weak output or demonstrate that the final reasoning is their own.

The program response

SAGE provides a six-step educational method through which students generate a starting point, evaluate AI output, refine it, respond to AI critique, reflect and defend their decisions.

Step 1GenerateEstablish a starting point.
Step 2EvaluateCheck against evidence.
Step 3RefineCorrect and improve.
Step 4AI CriticJudge the critique.
Step 5ReflectExplain the learning.
Step 6DefendDemonstrate ownership.

The program developed from an ICT classroom intervention into an integrated approach spanning assessment, research candidature and workplace practice. [C1]

Criterion C

C. Shown creativity, imagination and innovation

The innovation lies in teaching students to evaluate AI against disciplinary evidence and demonstrate ownership within the assessment itself. SAGE moves beyond permission and process logging by making judgement visible through the task. [C1]

1 · OpenGenerateUse AI after establishing the problem.
2 · OpenEvaluateCompare output with discipline evidence.
3 · OpenRefineAccept, reject or correct with reasons.
4 · OpenAI CriticAssess whether AI feedback is valid.
5 · OpenReflectArticulate what changed and why.
6 · AssuredDefendDemonstrate ownership under supervision.

SAGE Pedagogy

The core six-step process develops critical AI use through generation, evaluation, refinement, critique, reflection and defence.

SAGE-AP

Translates institutional AI permission settings into task-level requirements for verification, declaration and defence.

SAGE-R

Extends the assurance logic to research candidature, literature work and defensible authorship.

SAGE-I

Proposed professional-practice extension for transferring responsible AI judgement into workplace settings. Evidence and final naming to be confirmed.

Assurance by design

Evidence of individual understanding is designed into the assessment, rather than sought after concerns arise.

Assurance debt

When assurance is postponed or omitted, uncertainty about who owns the work accumulates and becomes harder to resolve.

Discipline-matched defence

The method of defence matches the kind of knowledge or performance the student must demonstrate.

Judgement demonstrated through the task

Students show how they evaluated and corrected AI output; logs or declarations alone are not treated as proof of learning.

Criterion D

D. Drawn on scholarly literature to inform the development of the program

SAGE developed through iterative empirical studies and learning and teaching scholarship rather than as an improvised response to AI. The map below connects established educational ideas with program design choices.

Closing statement

From an ICT response to a sustained program

SAGE began as an ICT educational response at CQUniversity and has developed into a sustained, evidence-based program with institutional, national and international application. The written evidence on this page is the primary route for assessing that development.
