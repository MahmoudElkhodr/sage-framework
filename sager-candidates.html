SAGE-R is an independent scholarly framework developed by CQUniversity researchers. This working draft is for consultation and is not currently an official CQUniversity policy or procedure.

Policy and permission boundary

SAGE-R does not independently authorise the use of generative AI. Any use must comply with applicable University policies, research ethics approvals, data-management and privacy requirements, intellectual-property arrangements, funder and publisher conditions, disciplinary standards, and supervisory guidance. Only institutionally approved tools and data-handling arrangements should be used where required.

Protect research information

Protect research data and intellectual property

Do not upload identifiable participant information, confidential datasets, culturally restricted information, commercially sensitive material, unpublished intellectual property, ethics documentation or third-party content to an AI service unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved. De-identification alone may not be sufficient where data-sharing agreements, Indigenous data sovereignty, contractual restrictions or re-identification risks apply.

Finding and checking literature

1
Finding and understanding literature
Using AI to find and summarise a research paper

Do not upload third-party papers or unpublished manuscripts unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved and content-sharing rights permit it. AI tools can help identify relevant papers and produce initial summaries of their content, saving considerable time in the early stages of a review. However, AI-generated summaries frequently contain errors — incorrect sample sizes, misattributed findings, fabricated statistics, and oversimplified interpretations are common. A summary you cannot verify is a liability, not an asset.

1

Use AI to produce an initial summary covering the research question, methodology, sample size, main findings, and stated limitations.

2

Verify every material claim that will inform the research. A three-item spot-check may be used as an initial reliability screen, but it does not replace direct reading and verification of the evidence ultimately relied upon.

3

If any detail is wrong, do not use the summary. Identify precisely what the AI misrepresented. Either read the relevant section and write your own notes, or, only where the tool and content-sharing arrangements are expressly approved, provide the relevant passage and request a correction based on that passage alone. Repeat the verification step.

4

The verified summary informs your own writing. It does not enter your thesis directly.

Defend check

Your supervisor may select any paper from your literature and ask you to describe its methodology and findings. If you can do this — including what the AI got wrong and how you corrected it — the process has worked as intended.

Writing a literature review

2
Writing from the literature
Using AI to help write the literature review

AI may assist with the prose of a literature review, but only for sources you have already screened, read, and understood. The literature review must reflect your scholarly judgement about what is relevant, how sources relate to one another, and what the field has and has not established. That judgement is the intellectual contribution the section is required to demonstrate.

1

Screen and select your sources yourself. You decide what is included and why. AI may assist with database searches and initial filtering, but inclusion decisions are yours.

2

Read the papers you intend to include. Apply the source summarisation process above. You must be able to explain what each source contributes and why it belongs.

3

Prepare your own analytical notes identifying the themes, debates, gaps, and connections you intend to discuss. These are yours.

4

AI may help convert your notes into prose using the Boundary Prompt Mechanism in Example 3. The output must not introduce claims or connections beyond what your notes established.

Important

Your supervisor may randomly select five references from your literature review and ask you to justify why each is included and what specific point it supports. If any reference was inserted by AI without your verification, this will be evident.

Defend check

Can you explain the thematic structure of your review, identify the key debates in the field, and justify the inclusion of any specific source without reading from AI-produced prose?

Writing from your reasoning

3
The Boundary Prompt Mechanism
Using AI to convert your dot points into research prose

Where AI-assisted drafting is permitted, the Boundary Prompt Mechanism provides a way to constrain assistance with writing sections of your thesis. A prompt does not guarantee compliance: you must verify the output and meet applicable disclosure requirements. Do not upload unpublished intellectual property or confidential notes without express approval of the tool and data handling. Your reasoning, evidence, and conclusions are established first — in your own notes — and AI is constrained to prose conversion only. The tool expresses your thinking; it does not generate new thinking.

1

Write your own dot points first. Include every claim you intend to make, the evidence that supports each one, the logical connections, and any qualifications. The depth of these notes determines the quality and accuracy of the output.

2

Construct a boundary prompt that explicitly instructs the tool not to add new claims, references, findings, or inferential steps beyond what you have provided.

3

Read the output closely. Check whether the AI has introduced additional claims, strengthened your qualifications, inserted new references, or drawn inferences you did not intend. These are common and must be removed.

4

Revise and own the final version. The text that enters your thesis must be one you fully endorse and can Defend.

Boundary Prompt Template
"Write the following section using only the points and evidence listed below. Do not add new claims, introduce additional references, draw conclusions beyond what the points establish, or strengthen any qualification I have expressed. Maintain the logical sequence as given. Use formal academic prose appropriate to a doctoral thesis."

[Your dot points here]

After receiving the output: highlight any sentence that introduces something not present in your dot points and remove or rewrite it.

Defend check

Can you show your original dot points and demonstrate that the final prose did not extend beyond them? Can you explain every claim in the section and identify the evidence that supports it?

Coding, methods and instruments

4
Discipline methods support
Using AI for coding, experiments, and discipline-specific methods

Research involves substantial technical execution tasks that are not in themselves knowledge contributions. Writing and debugging code, constructing simulation models, designing laboratory protocols, producing circuit diagrams, building survey instruments, generating patient assessment frameworks, and constructing financial models all involve labour that AI can assist with effectively. Such assistance may be appropriate where permitted by disciplinary and institutional requirements. Do not upload protected code, unpublished models, survey or interview instruments, participant information or confidential protocols unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved. The requirement is that you understand what the tool has produced and can explain and justify every methodological decision it embodies.

What AI assistance with methods looks like

A social science student uses AI to generate an interview guide from their research questions. An engineering student uses AI to debug a simulation script. A nursing student uses AI to structure a systematic review protocol. A business student uses AI to generate initial survey items from their theoretical framework.

What you must own

The methodological rationale — why this approach, why these questions, why this design. AI can produce an interview guide; it cannot decide whether semi-structured interviews are the appropriate method for your research question. That decision is entirely yours.

Defend check

Your supervisor may ask what tools you used, how outputs were verified, and what the code or protocol does, why it is designed that way, and what would change if a specific design decision were altered.

Analysis and interpretation

5
Analysis and findings
Using AI to assist with data analysis and results interpretation

Statistical and analytical software has been central to research for decades. Running a t-test, ANOVA, regression, or thematic coding procedure in SPSS, R, NVivo, or Python does not make analysis less rigorous — the tool applies established procedures to your data and you interpret the result. Where permitted, AI may assist with analytical work, but its outputs require careful verification. Do not upload confidential datasets or participant information unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved; de-identification alone may be insufficient.

The similarity to SPSS

Conventional statistical software generally executes a specified analytical procedure deterministically; the researcher remains responsible for data quality, test selection, assumptions, parameter settings and interpretation.

The critical difference

Generative AI may introduce errors, omit relevant patterns, apply silent assumptions, or produce plausible-sounding interpretations that are statistically or contextually wrong. You cannot trust a result because it looks reasonable. You must verify that it is correct.

1

You decide the analytical approach. The choice of test, coding strategy, or analytical framework is a methodological decision that must precede and remain independent of AI assistance.

2

Use AI to assist with execution — running the analysis, producing visualisations, organising output, and generating a first-pass interpretation.

3

Scrutinise the output for four specific risks: factual errors in the numbers; patterns that have been overlooked; assumptions about your data the tool applied without stating; and interpretations that reflect training data rather than your specific research context.

4

The interpretation and its justification are yours. AI may produce a plausible narrative of your results. You must determine whether that narrative is accurate and appropriate to your context before it enters your thesis.

Defend check

In your progress meeting, your supervisor may ask you to walk through a specific finding: why you chose the analytical method, what the result shows, what alternative explanations exist, and what the result does not show.

Discussion and implications

6
Discussion, implications, and recommendations
Using AI in the interpretive and contribution sections

These sections are where your original contribution is most visible. They require you to synthesise your findings with the existing literature, identify what your study adds to or challenges in the field, acknowledge limitations honestly, and draw conclusions proportionate to your evidence. AI may assist with drafting using the Boundary Prompt Mechanism, but four verification responsibilities apply here that do not apply to more procedural sections.

1

Verify against overgeneralisation. AI-generated discussion prose frequently overstates significance or suppresses qualifications. Check every claim against your actual results and ask whether the strength of the language is justified.

2

Check for cultural and contextual assumptions. AI tools may embed assumptions drawn from international contexts that do not reflect your study's setting — a healthcare recommendation grounded in US or European practice may not apply to the Australian or regional context your research addresses. Identify and correct these mismatches.

3

Confirm every implication is grounded in your study. AI may generate plausible implications relevant to your topic but not supported by your specific findings. Every recommendation must be traceable to a result or pattern in your data.

4

Apply your domain knowledge. Your understanding of the context in which the study was conducted, and your familiarity with the practical environment your recommendations address, are not things AI possesses. They are what make the discussion an original scholarly contribution.

Defend check

An examiner may challenge any recommendation by asking: what specific finding supports this, how does it fit the existing literature, and what are its limitations in your specific context?

References and citations

7
Reference management
Using AI to manage and generate references

Reference management software such as Endnote, Zotero, and Mendeley has automated reference formatting and insertion for many years. AI assistance with reference management occupies the same functional category — it is a productivity tool that handles a mechanical task, and may be appropriate where permitted by applicable requirements. Formatting references manually is not a research skill. Verifying that references are accurate is.

AI carries one additional and significant risk that reference management software does not: it will generate plausible-looking but entirely fabricated references. A paper with a convincing title, plausible authors, and a real journal name may simply not exist. Verification in this area is absolute.

1

Where permitted, use AI or reference software for formatting, organising, and inserting references. Use tools and data-handling arrangements approved for the material involved.

2

Verify bibliographic details using an authoritative database and confirm the cited claim against the original publication. Discovery tools can help locate a source; they do not replace reading it.

3

Never cite a source you cannot locate. If the paper does not appear in a reputable database, the reference must be removed regardless of how plausible it appears.

Note for supervisors

AI may assist in organising a reference-checking workflow, but existence and bibliographic accuracy must be confirmed through authoritative databases and the original publication.

Defend check

Your supervisor or examiner may select any reference and ask you to locate the source and confirm the accuracy of your citation. If every reference has been verified, this presents no difficulty.

Ethics and disclosure

AI assistance with the structure or language of an ethics application may be appropriate where permitted. Every ethical judgement—risks, mitigation, consent and the participant experience—must remain yours.

Keep protected material out of unapproved tools

Do not upload ethics documentation, participant information, survey or interview instruments, confidential protocols or unpublished intellectual property unless the tool and proposed data handling have been expressly approved.

Follow the disclosure and record-retention requirements of your institution, ethics approval, project, funder and publisher. Your supervisor or examiner may ask which tools you used, what they contributed and how you verified the results.

Preparing to Defend

Be ready to explain the decisions behind your work, using the sources and evidence you actually relied upon.

  • Explain the research problem and why it matters.
  • Justify your methodology and the alternatives you considered.
  • Locate the original evidence for every material claim.
  • Explain where AI assisted, what you checked and what you corrected.
  • Identify the limits of your findings and conclusions.
  • Meet the applicable disclosure and research-information requirements.
The student rule

If you cannot verify it, explain it and Defend it, it is not ready to enter your research.
